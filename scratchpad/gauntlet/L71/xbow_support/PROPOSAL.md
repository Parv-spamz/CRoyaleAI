# Dead-lane X-Bow support: evidence and bounded correction

Recorded before implementation. The owner authorizes implementation without another proposal review.

The audit links113 of114 dead-lane pro X-Bows.51 can reach a crown tower;57 encounter a ground target during their observed lives; only8 have neither. Nearby support is not a reliable waste label:141 nearby plays spend372 elixir, and28 occur without a target currently in reach, but only2 precede opposite-lane pressure within6 seconds and neither accompanies opposite-princess HP loss in that window. Saved live frames contain no dead-lane examples. The owner's reported failure is credible but this archive does not establish its causal mechanism. The current audit has no valid counterfactual result.

## Implementation

1. Add an offline expert-context cohort spanning the observed lifetime of these dead-lane X-Bows, including useful and low-value examples, expert WAIT rows and all expert actions. Retain every target and the train/held-out replay split. Give this rare context a bounded5% training mixture, replacing5% of ordinary rows. This is an exposure experiment, not a rule telling the model to preserve or abandon a bow. The ordinary pool retains active-lane and other defensive contexts as controls.
2. Train an explicit ablation against the same recipe without that5% cohort. Compare held-out gate/card/cell agreement within the cohort and globally, plus same-code ghost/reactive wins and support-spending telemetry. Do not adopt a candidate merely because it spends less near X-Bows; useful protection and crown reach must remain distinct.
3. Add public live audit snapshots with own hand/elixir, tower state, body identities and confirmed decisions. Capture the next opposite-lane threat and tower HP outcome. Preserve source and timing coverage; lack of frames is unknown. This closes the actual evidence gap without creating a placement ban or hardcoded support budget.
4. Use recorded simulator states to compare candidate support and withholding at matched decision points, retaining the same opponent policy and subsequent model decisions. Charge all later elixir spending, not just the initial card. Keep simulator counterfactuals separate from live outcomes. This remains an acceptance task; no counterfactual benefit is claimed yet.

All tactical decisions remain learned. The public observation correction is independent of this sampling ablation. If the exposure experiment fails its acceptance criteria, retain the accepted policy and the new audit coverage rather than inventing a tactical prohibition.
