## R3 bandit finding (measured, contradicts ticket text -- reported per CLAUDE.md disagreement policy)

Ticket text: "Dashes (bandit, ram, prince charge) are covered by the cap [0.25]." Computed directly:
`CardDB.speed_tiles('bandit') == 1.5` tiles/s (its baseline move speed; cards_stats.json/card_mechanics.json
have `dash_time_s`/`dash_damage` for bandit but NO dash-speed-tiles field -- the burst speed during a dash
is not in this data at all). With the ticket's own formula, axis choice, and constants:
`speed_norm = 1.5/18 = 0.0833/s`; at a typical estimator-cadence sample `dt=0.25s`:
`raw = 0.0833 * 0.25 * 1.5 = 0.0313`; `match_radius_eff = max(0.07, 0.0313) = 0.07` -- UNCHANGED from the
plain `match_radius`, because bandit's own card speed never drives the term above the floor, let alone the
0.25 cap. This is arithmetically incapable of covering the trace's OWN measured bandit rebill distances
(charges.jsonl `rebill_out_of_radius`, base=bandit, n=7, `prev_same_base_min_dist` = [0.1032, 0.1126, 0.114,
0.1147, 0.1201, 0.1252, 0.1278], median 0.1147) -- 0.07 < all seven. CONCLUSION: R3, implemented exactly as
specified against `CardDB.speed_tiles`, does NOT close bandit's real over-billing; the ticket's "covered by
the cap" claim is CONTRADICTED by the data, not merely untested. Root cause: `speed_tiles` is the card's
baseline move speed, not its ability-boosted dash speed, and no dash-speed field exists in this DB for
bandit/ram_rider/prince to feed the formula instead. Implemented literally per spec regardless (source =
`db.speed_tiles`, matching the ticket's explicit instruction to convert "cards_stats `speed_tiles`"); the
required R3 unit test below uses a stub DB with an explicit higher speed value (documented inline) to
demonstrate the MECHANISM works when given a large-enough speed input, since real bandit data cannot
demonstrate it. Flagging for the lead: closing bandit's real rebill needs either a manually-sourced
dash-speed table (out of scope here -- would be guessed, not measured) or a uniform K large enough to matter,
which would widen match_radius for every ordinary (non-dashing) card too.
